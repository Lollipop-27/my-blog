# Project 3 — Weekly Weblog: UX of Compost

## Week 5: Rethinking Our Relationship with Compost

### Field Research and Personal Perspectives

**Monday, October 19**

We visited Calthorpe Community Garden to observe a composting session. I had expected to observe the process rather than participate in a workshop, so we stayed for approximately half an hour. Although our visit was brief, it gave us an opportunity to encounter composting in a community setting.

Afterwards, we agreed to collect love letters and breakup letters addressed to compost, using personal perspectives to explore people's relationships with it. I asked my mum to share photographs of our garden's compost bin, along with her own letters. This introduced a personal perspective to our research and connected the project to composting practices at home.

### Exploring the Creative Direction

**Tuesday, October 20**

Our group discussed creating something with slime, potentially in the form of an educational video. However, our tutorial with Tonisha made us question whether this approach would genuinely help people learn about compost or whether we were mainly attracted to slime because of its visual appeal and popularity.

We also wanted to collect perspectives from younger people. I asked my nephew to write a letter to compost, and we recorded videos or audio of our family members reading their letters aloud. These recordings allowed us to explore how different people express their relationships with compost.

### Feedback and Reframing the Concept

**Thursday, October 22**

During our desktop conversation with Ali and Alex, we received feedback that slime might not be the most appropriate direction. They encouraged us to move away from presenting compost through human emotions and instead consider what compost itself could teach us.

This shifted our thinking from expressing feelings about compost towards creating an experience through which people could learn about it by interacting with it. Afterwards, we brainstormed possible creative toolkit research methods. One idea was to create a building representing different living conditions, allowing us to explore how people's environments influence their relationship with compost.

### Developing the Creative Toolkit

**Friday, October 23**

We continued discussing the creative toolkit during a group call. We explored different settings where people could encounter compost, including a room containing compost or a dinner table where compost became part of an everyday domestic environment.

These discussions encouraged us to consider how the surrounding environment could shape people's perceptions and interactions with compost. Rather than simply explaining composting through information, we began exploring how we could create situations that encourage people to think differently about it.
