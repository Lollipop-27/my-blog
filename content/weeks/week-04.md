# Project 2 — Weekly Weblog: UX of Latency

## Week 4: Exploring Interrelation

### Developing the Concept

**Monday, October 12**

Following our behavioural mapping at different retail checkouts, we met to discuss how we could translate our observations into a physical experience. We revisited what we had learned about latency, particularly how delays can interrupt a task and affect the behaviour of others.

We experimented with bodystorming using strings to represent connections between different elements. This helped us explore how people, objects, and systems depend on one another. We became interested in the idea of **interrelation**: how one action or interruption can create a chain reaction and affect the wider experience. Rather than focusing only on the person experiencing a delay, we wanted to explore how that delay might influence other participants and their ability to complete a task. This became the main direction for our final prototype.

### Prototyping and Testing

**Tuesday, October 13**

We discussed possible directions for our final prototype and settled on a cup-stacking race. During our tutorial, Tonisha suggested introducing randomisation to make the experience less predictable. We decided to use a dice, with each number corresponding to a different type of latency associated with a self-checkout kiosk.

We also tested the activity with classmates to check whether the instructions and interactions were clear.

**Wednesday, October 14**

I worked on writing the instructions for the final prototype, focusing on explaining the rules and how different latency scenarios would interrupt the participants' tasks.

### Presentation Feedback

**Thursday, October 15**

The tutors suggested that our prototype could have been more experimental. Instead of using familiar objects such as cups, we could have explored unusual materials, such as sticky or oversized objects, to create a more unexpected physical experience. They also noted that our group roles were not clearly defined, highlighting the importance of communication and task distribution during collaborative design.

We were also introduced to our next project, **UX of Compost**. Since some group members were unfamiliar with composting, I suggested that everyone conduct individual research and share their findings on FigJam to establish a common understanding of the topic.

### Initial Research into Composting

**Saturday, October 17**

While researching composting, I noticed a difference between my experience of composting education in Canada and its visibility in my everyday life in London. Growing up in Canada, composting was introduced in school, whereas I had encountered less public promotion of it in the UK.

This led me to consider whether housing conditions might affect composting habits. People living in flats may have less access to outdoor space than those living in houses with gardens. Although this was only an initial hypothesis, it gave me a starting point for exploring how living environments, infrastructure, and education influence people's relationship with composting.
